### ATLB-08 — Relaxation

<sub>Algorithms to Live By</sub>

- **ATLB-08-R1** — **Establish whether the problem is tractable before committing effort to an exact answer.** Difficulty is a measurable property, and many ordinary problems have no efficient solution.
- **ATLB-08-R2** — **Treat intractability as a change of method, not as defeat.** The response is to relax, not to abandon or to grind.
- **ATLB-08-R3** — **Solve the problem you wish you had first**, then bring the constraints back. If you cannot solve what is in front of you, solve an easier version and see whether it offers a starting point.
- **ATLB-08-R4** — **Use the relaxed answer as a bound.** Knowing the best conceivable outcome tells you how far from optimal your practical answer is, without ever finding the optimum.
- **ATLB-08-R5** — **Ask the fantasy questions deliberately** — what would you do without the fear, the money constraint, the travel time — as an analytical step rather than as consolation.
- **ATLB-08-R6** — **When forced into either/or, imagine the blend, then round.** A fractional solution is not an answer, but converting it back is usually cheap and often provably close.
- **ATLB-08-R7** — **Convert probabilities into decisions where averages cannot apply**, rather than discarding a fractional result as meaningless.
- **ATLB-08-R8** — **Accept a bounded multiple of optimal.** Twice the optimal number of invitations or vaccinations, computed quickly, beats an unattainable perfect answer.
- **ATLB-08-R9** — **Ask "or else what?" of every hard constraint.** Turning a prohibition into a priced penalty is what makes an intractable problem tractable.
- **ATLB-08-R10** — **Audit which constraints are genuinely inviolable.** Stated absolutes frequently turn out to have been violated repeatedly and forgotten — check the record before designing around them.
- **ATLB-08-R11** — **Consider paying a known penalty rather than accepting a large loss** to satisfy a rule; and note that merely imagining the infraction clarifies the real cost.
- **ATLB-08-R12** — **Expect a good process to surface options nobody proposed.** The value of optimisation on an intractable problem is often the unconsidered arrangement, not the optimum.
- **ATLB-08-R13** — **Distinguish conscious from unconscious wishful thinking.** Relaxation works because it is designed to be reconciled with reality; fantasy that is never reconciled ends in the collapse Booker describes.

### ATLB-12 — Computational Kindness

<sub>Algorithms to Live By</sub>

- **ATLB-12-R5** — **Choose tractable problems where you have the choice.** Complexity awareness is a filter on which situations to enter, not only a method for handling them.
- **ATLB-12-R16** — **Stop treating assumption, approximation and chance as compromises.** On hard problems they are what rationality consists of.
