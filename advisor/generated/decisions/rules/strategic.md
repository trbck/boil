### ATLB-11 — Game Theory

<sub>Algorithms to Live By</sub>

- **ATLB-11-R1** — **Stop escalating levels of "what do they think I think."** Simulating a system as complex as yourself exhausts your resources by definition.
- **ATLB-11-R2** — **Play one level above your opponent, not more.** Over-modelling attributes information to people who do not have it, and makes your own signals unreadable.
- **ATLB-11-R3** — **Watch for opponents who profit from your deliberation.** Where thinking is costly and time-bounded, an adversary can win by giving you nothing worth thinking about.
- **ATLB-11-R4** — **Compute the equilibrium instead of reading the person** where one is available — but treat "an equilibrium exists" as no guarantee anyone can find it.
- **ATLB-11-R5** — **Discount predictions built on equilibria that are intractable to compute.** If it cannot be found, it cannot be relied on as a forecast of behaviour.
- **ATLB-11-R6** — **Never assume a stable outcome is a good one.** Stability is what makes bad equilibria resistant, not what makes them desirable.
- **ATLB-11-R7** — **Prefer dominant strategies where they exist** — they remove the need to model anyone else at all.
- **ATLB-11-R8** — **Estimate the price of anarchy before demanding coordination.** Sometimes central control adds only a third; sometimes its absence is ruinous.
- **ATLB-11-R9** — **Recognise the commons structure**: private gain, diffuse harm, and a race that leaves everyone worse off with no relative advantage gained.
- **ATLB-11-R10** — **Do not try to fix a bad equilibrium from inside it.** Change the rules, or find an outside authority who can.
- **ATLB-11-R11** — **Making outcomes worse can make everyone better off** if it moves the equilibrium. Constraints are not merely costs.
- **ATLB-11-R12** — **Sweetening a payoff without moving the equilibrium accomplishes nothing.** Check whether your incentive changes the *structure* or merely the stakes.
- **ATLB-11-R13** — **Set floors rather than offering rewards** in races to the bottom — a compulsory minimum costs nothing and works where a bonus fails.
- **ATLB-11-R14** — **Bind yourself deliberately.** Contracts, schedules, closing times and commitments are equilibrium-shifting devices, not just restrictions.
- **ATLB-11-R15** — **Treat involuntary commitment as an asset.** A reputation for responding disproportionately deters exploitation; predictability of feeling substitutes for external enforcement.
- **ATLB-11-R16** — **Beware when public information exceeds private information** — when you know what others are doing but not why, you may be joining a cascade rather than learning.
- **ATLB-11-R17** — **Broadcast your doubts even when you act with the crowd**, so your action does not read as confidence and stop being informative.
- **ATLB-11-R18** — **Remember that a cascade requires no irrationality or malice.** Assigning blame after a collapse may simply be the wrong frame.
- **ATLB-11-R19** — **Design for truthfulness.** If you do not want people optimising against you, optimise for them — any game requiring strategic misrepresentation can in principle be rebuilt so that honesty dominates.
- **ATLB-11-R20** — **Count strategising as a cost of the game itself**, and prefer arrangements that let you act on what you actually want.
