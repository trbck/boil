### ATLB-05 — Scheduling

<sub>Algorithms to Live By</sub>

- **ATLB-05-R1** — **Choose the metric before choosing the schedule.** Without one, no ordering is better than another, and conflicting productivity advice cannot be adjudicated.
- **ATLB-05-R2** — **To minimise the worst delay, work by earliest due date** — and note that task durations are irrelevant to this rule, so you need not estimate them.
- **ATLB-05-R3** — **To minimise how many things are late, drop the largest scheduled item the moment you fall behind**, and stop worrying about the order of anything already late.
- **ATLB-05-R4** — **To shrink a backlog fastest, do the shortest task first.**
- **ATLB-05-R5** — **To act on what matters, order by importance divided by duration** — only prioritise something twice as long if it is twice as important.
- **ATLB-05-R6** — **Decide whether you are minimising the amount or the count** before choosing a strategy — it is the difference between the debt avalanche and the debt snowball, and neither is universally right.
- **ATLB-05-R7** — **Read procrastination as a metric error, not a character flaw.** Clearing trivia is optimal behaviour for minimising task *count*; fix the objective rather than the discipline.
- **ATLB-05-R8** — **Audit the metrics your tools impose on you.** Unread badges silently weight everything equally; if you cannot change what they count, remove them.
- **ATLB-05-R9** — **Watch for priority inversion** — if the important thing is stalled, find what is blocking it, and promote the blocker to the priority of the thing it blocks.
- **ATLB-05-R10** — **Treat a blocking trivial task as the most important task.** "Never let what matters most be at the mercy of what matters least" is false under precedence constraints.
- **ATLB-05-R11** — **Schedule back to front when tasks have prerequisites**, placing last the unblocked task with the latest due date.
- **ATLB-05-R12** — **Expect most real scheduling problems to be intractable** — 84% of classified ones are — and use these rules as good starting points rather than expecting optimality.
- **ATLB-05-R13** — **Prefer reacting to planning when the future is uncertain.** Foreknowledge can make the optimal schedule uncomputable while on-the-fly rules stay easy and nearly as good.
- **ATLB-05-R14** — **Count context switches as pure loss.** Interrupting someone more than a few times an hour can reduce their output to nothing.
- **ATLB-05-R15** — **Protect blocks long enough to reload context** — for demanding work, anything under about ninety minutes is largely spent on reloading.
- **ATLB-05-R16** — **Recognise thrashing by its signature**: full effort, no progress, and no spare capacity to reorganise. Performance collapses rather than degrading.
- **ATLB-05-R17** — **When thrashing, lower the quality of your prioritisation, not your effort.** Choosing what to do next can cost more than doing it; arbitrary order beats paralysis.
- **ATLB-05-R18** — **Refuse work whose working set will not fit**, before the collapse rather than after.
- **ATLB-05-R19** — **Set a minimum time slice** and honour it, so that responsiveness cannot consume throughput entirely.
- **ATLB-05-R20** — **Be exactly as responsive as required and no more**, then batch everything else — coalesce bills, mail and messages into scheduled passes rather than servicing each interrupt on arrival.
