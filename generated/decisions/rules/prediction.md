### ATLB-06 — Bayes's Rule

<sub>Algorithms to Live By</sub>

- **ATLB-06-R1** — **Identify the distribution before choosing a prediction rule.** Multiplicative, Average and Additive give contradictory answers; nearly all single-observation errors are shape errors, not arithmetic errors.
- **ATLB-06-R2** — **Use the Multiplicative Rule for scale-free quantities** — grosses, wealth, city sizes, durations of unfamiliar things. The longer it has run, the longer to expect.
- **ATLB-06-R3** — **Use the Average Rule for quantities with a natural scale** — predict the mean, and a little beyond once the mean is passed.
- **ATLB-06-R4** — **Use the Additive Rule for memoryless processes** — predict a constant increment, and stop looking for the right moment to quit, because there isn't one.
- **ATLB-06-R5** — **Apply Laplace's Law, (w+1)/(n+2), for a rate from few trials.** One success from one attempt is 2/3, not certainty.
- **ATLB-06-R6** — **Never quote a median without the shape of its tail.** A right-skewed distribution turns "half die within eight months" into a very different personal forecast.
- **ATLB-06-R7** — **Use "it will last as long as it has lasted" only when you genuinely know nothing** — including not knowing the timescale. Where you have real knowledge, this rule is actively misleading.
- **ATLB-06-R8** — **Calibrate surprise to the distribution.** Under a power law, be *most* alarmed when a long-running thing has run longest; venerability is not safety.
- **ATLB-06-R9** — **State your prior explicitly, even as a guess.** Bayes cannot run without one, and refusing to name it does not remove it.
- **ATLB-06-R10** — **Reverse-engineer priors from predictions.** What someone expects reveals the distribution their experience taught them, and is measurable where direct data is not.
- **ATLB-06-R11** — **Distrust your predictions in domains where you lack exposure.** Failure is not an inference error but an absent prior.
- **ATLB-06-R12** — **Read apparent impatience as a possible forecast, not a character defect.** Giving up early is rational when waits are scale-free or the source is unreliable.
- **ATLB-06-R13** — **Treat reliability as an input to other people's patience.** Consistently keeping promises changes what others can rationally afford to wait for.
- **ATLB-06-R14** — **Guard the inputs that form your priors.** Media frequency is not world frequency; a diet of the unusual will systematically miscalibrate you.
