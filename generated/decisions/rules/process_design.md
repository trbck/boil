### ATLB-12 — Computational Kindness

<sub>Algorithms to Live By</sub>

- **ATLB-12-R1** — **Evaluate decisions by the process, not the outcome.** A provably optimal method still fails often; if you followed the best available process, the loss is not a mistake.
- **ATLB-12-R2** — **State the failure rate of your method up front.** 37% optimal means 63% failure, and knowing that in advance is what makes a bad outcome bearable rather than evidence against the method.
- **ATLB-12-R3** — **Recognise that even perfect information would not guarantee success** in many of these problems — the ceiling is lower than intuition assumes.
- **ATLB-12-R4** — **Accept "good enough" on intractable problems** rather than paying unbounded cost for an answer you cannot reach.
- **ATLB-12-R5** — **Choose tractable problems where you have the choice.** Complexity awareness is a filter on which situations to enter, not only a method for handling them.
- **ATLB-12-R6** — **Treat every request you make as a computational problem you are imposing**, and minimise its cost — this is the core move.
- **ATLB-12-R7** — **Prefer verification to search when asking something of someone.** A concrete proposal to accept or decline is far cheaper than an open-ended question.
- **ATLB-12-R8** — **Offer constrained options, even somewhat arbitrary ones.** "Tuesday 1–2pm" gets more yeses than "any time that works."
- **ATLB-12-R9** — **State your preferences rather than withholding them.** "I'm flexible" passes the cognitive buck and forces others to simulate your mind — the most expensive computation there is.
- **ATLB-12-R10** — **Reduce the number of options you present**, and let others eliminate rather than construct.
- **ATLB-12-R11** — **Beware of consensus assembled from mutual inference.** Groups reach decisions nobody wanted when each person mirrors the enthusiasm they read in the others.
- **ATLB-12-R12** — **Design so the greedy algorithm stays optimal.** A slightly worse arrangement that keeps the decision simple usually beats an optimal one that makes every user solve a hard problem.
- **ATLB-12-R13** — **Audit designs for the mental labour they impose**, alongside space, materials and money — the cost is real and rarely counted.
- **ATLB-12-R14** — **Replace spinning with blocking wherever the waiting is done by people.** Notify rather than making people watch; convert a continuous re-decision into a single one.
- **ATLB-12-R15** — **Publish the information that collapses a repeated inference into one decision** — a countdown, an ETA, a confirmed time.
- **ATLB-12-R16** — **Stop treating assumption, approximation and chance as compromises.** On hard problems they are what rationality consists of.
